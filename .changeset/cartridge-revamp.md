**Cartridge** is now a late-90s 64-bit console all the way through, not just a charcoal palette. The keys follow the controller: primary is the blue A key, danger is the red Start key (it rumbles when pressed), the tile key is the yellow C key, all domed and lit from above. Big titles are Titan One with a gold fill and an extruded edge. Dialogs, popovers, toasts and tooltips are see-through text boxes, toasts wait on a blinking red arrow, and dialogs zoom in out of the fog. Every card is a cartridge: a plastic shell with grip ridges across the top and the label set into it, and interactive ones tip toward you on hover. Primary, secondary and tile keys carry a round A, B or C prompt, and focus is a breathing yellow lock-on. On its own, the mosaic becomes a stage floor tipped back in perspective, each tile a raised block. The page sits on a checkerboard floor that fades into distance fog; progress is a magic meter, the slider thumb is an analog stick, the switch is the power slider, accent badges are coins and avatars are shaded spheres. The accent moves from green to A-key blue, and new `key-a`, `key-b`, `key-c` and `key-start` tokens (with `on-*` pairs) hold the controller colors. Display type changes from Lilita One to Titan One, so reload `themes/cartridge-fonts.css`.
